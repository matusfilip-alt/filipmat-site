![Spires of St. Wenceslas Cathedral in Olomouc seen through a barbed-wire fence at dusk](/media/hometown/olomouc-1.jpg)

It never gets old. Olomouc, a city dating back to the 11th century, has so many unique places that get overlooked. Take the Caesar Fountain on the Upper Square. Julius Caesar rides a rearing horse, his back to the Town Hall. Look up at the roof of the pink house at number 15 and you will spot a row of little figures, like his senators, watching over the square with him. Most people walk past without noticing.

![Crowd of people walking across the cobbled square in Olomouc, a carousel in the background](/media/hometown/olomouc-17.jpg)

Olomouc has seen a lot. It held off the Mongols in 1241 and the Prussian army in 1758. It did not survive everything, though. During the Thirty Years’ War, Swedish troops occupied it for eight years and left it in ruins. And yet it is still standing, changing, improving, and vibing.

![Weathered stone wall of an old Olomouc house with boarded-up windows and a dark doorway](/media/hometown/olomouc-7.jpg)

Sometimes I find beauty in imperfection. I am not saying it is easy to create promo photos of the city. I just do not see it that way. The less pretty corners, the old imperfect buildings, the weird views: that is what keeps catching my eye.

I am sure you know the feeling. Even though you have walked the same street a thousand times, you can always find something new.

![Narrow stone staircase between high brick walls leading up to a cobbled street](/media/hometown/olomouc-4.jpg)

By walking, I mean walking and watching. It is not always doable. Sometimes your head is spinning, the kids are screaming, you are angry at someone, or someone sees you as a target. In those moments, it is better to just find the shortest way from A to B.

![Man with a backpack walking away through a busy Christmas market, seen from behind](/media/hometown/olomouc-13.jpg)

![Priests and people in black coats among the crowd on the square in Olomouc](/media/hometown/olomouc-14.jpg)

![Group of nuns and priests in black standing on the cobblestones of the square](/media/hometown/olomouc-15.jpg)

But sometimes your head is clear, your mind is rested or in offline mode, and you see things you have ignored for years.

![Strings of lights in bare park trees at dusk, seen past a dark window frame](/media/hometown/olomouc-3.jpg)

![Bare young tree in front of a tall wooden door behind an iron fence](/media/hometown/olomouc-8.jpg)

![Path through fallen leaves in an overgrown park slope in Olomouc](/media/hometown/olomouc-9.jpg)

If you are lucky and your camera is with you, there is a good chance you will capture the moment. Someone might see it as ugly or uninteresting. But to me it always feels special, because it changes the way I see things I have seen a million times.

![Shopping centre facade with Luxor and Billa signs, a street lamp and the Holy Trinity Column behind it](/media/hometown/olomouc-12.jpg)

![Girl with glasses drawing with markers at a table, framed through a window](/media/hometown/olomouc-11.jpg)

![Spiral bicycle stand and a fallen e-scooter on the cobbles in front of a post office](/media/hometown/olomouc-16.jpg)

So next time you cross the square, slow down for a moment. Look up. Caesar and his senators have been waiting for centuries. They can wait a little longer for you to notice them.

![Cathedral spires rising above a dark road with car headlights at dusk](/media/hometown/olomouc-20.jpg)

![Empty Olomouc street at night lit by street lamps](/media/hometown/olomouc-19.jpg)

![Overgrown old villa behind a hedge on a grey day](/media/hometown/olomouc-15-5.jpg)

It is a good feeling to walk around the city, be present, and be part of it for a moment.
