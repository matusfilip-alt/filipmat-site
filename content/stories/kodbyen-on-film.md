Copenhagen is a very colorful city. That is why I took my old Leica Mini, a compact film camera from 1991, and loaded it with a roll of black and white Ilford 400. There is never much time to see a city on a work trip, but I do not see the point in waiting three hours at the airport before a flight. So yes, the trip home is sometimes a little stressful. My heart rate goes up, I am usually the last one boarding, and I sprint through the terminal. But it is worth it. At least for me.

A while ago, a friend recommended Kødbyen, the Meatpacking District in Vesterbro, just a short walk from Central Station. It was not built to be cool. It was built to keep people healthy. In the 19th century, butchers worked all over Copenhagen, in backyards and cellars. After the cholera epidemic of 1853, the city decided to move all of that off the streets and into one place it could control. The Brown Meat City opened in 1879 with its red brick halls and cattle market. In the 1930s came the White Meat City: functionalist buildings covered in white ceramic tiles, because white tiles were easy to wash and showed every bit of dirt.

Today it is a cultural hub. Theatres, clubs, galleries, restaurants, shops, breweries, and even a gym. And here is the funny part: some meat businesses still work there, so a butcher's van can park right next to a bar where people queue for natural wine.

Mikkeller and Warpigs were the places I wanted to visit and taste. Warpigs is a brewpub Mikkeller opened together with the American brewery Three Floyds, with beer brewed on site and Texas-style barbecue. In a former meat market. It makes sense.

![Storefront of Warpigs and Mikkeller with tall windows and a bicycle parked outside](/media/kodbyen-on-film/kodbyen-01.jpg)

![People eating at long tables inside a tiled former meat hall, light coming through the windows](/media/kodbyen-on-film/kodbyen-02.jpg)

The weather was typical Danish autumn. Rainy and cold. But the quick trip was great. Do not get me wrong, the place is not posh or shiny. But it has a certain coolness and a great vibe. You can feel the stories in the walls, and I believe those white tiles have witnessed a lot of interesting situations. First the work of the butchers, now the long nights of people who come to enjoy life.

On the way back to the train and the airport, I took a few snaps just to finish the roll. I could not wait to get it developed.

There was no plan or structure. As always. Well, to be honest, there kind of was: to see the place, enjoy the moment, and switch off for a second. As I said, it was worth it. Even though the airport staff were not nice to me and my calves were burning the whole flight back to Kraków.
